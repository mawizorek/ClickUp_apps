## 📏 Measured grain — Big Love (Charles L. Mee), 99 pages

| Metric | Cold estimate | After 60 pages read | **ACTUAL** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spine units | 40–70 | ~28 | **29** (+ U00) |
| Demands | 150–300 | ~65 | far above 65 — U28 alone carries a dozen |
| Pages | ~120 | ~120 | **99** |
| Stage-direction lines | ~400 | ~675 | ~675 (5.6/page avg) |

⭐ **The estimate made after reading real pages was good; the one made from zero pages was 2× wrong in both directions.** Count a sample before quoting a number.

⚠️ **Stage-direction line counts are a LINEATION artifact, not a density fact.** Mee writes one thought per line, verse-style; a prose playwright writes the same content in a quarter of the rows. A conventional script lands nearer 150–250. **Mee is close to the canon's worst case.**

⚠️ **Density is wildly uneven.** Big Love part 1 ran 8.5 stage-direction lines/page (the opening is a world-building block); part 3 ran 2.7. **Never extrapolate from the front of a script.**

## 🩹 Scars — every one cost something

### S1 · A partial read is wrong about SCALE, not just coverage
A 31-demand seed list drawn from pp. 1–20 and 41–60 **missed the two largest physical demands in the play**, because both sat in the 20 pages not yet read (the helicopter at p. 29, the ground-throwing aria at pp. 37–40). The whole build was priced off that sample. **A ledger from some of the pages is not a small version of the real one.**

### S2 · The cast sheet overrides the script's cast list
Mee's front matter doubles `Bella/Eleanor` and `Piero/Leo`. The quick change was flagged twice, including into a durable comment on a live production task — **after the show had cast eleven actors in eleven roles.** A slash is an OPTION the production already ruled on. ⭐ **The correction inverts cleanly: the script's doubling became a `waived` demand with the reason recorded. A correction is DATA, not just an erasure.**

### S3 · Never assert a coverage gap without reading the Contact Sheet
Wrote that intimacy direction was "not on anyone's radar" 18 days from first rehearsal. **Sara Penner was already seated as Intimacy and Acting Coach — and already named in Milo's own org chart.** The failure was reasoning from the SCRIPT toward the production instead of reading the production.

### S4 · …and the same field catches the opposite error
Hours later, nearly closed that same Contact Sheet's `Scenic Designer / NEED` row because Michael described the plan. **He had said "likely," not "confirmed."** The sheet was correctly holding uncertainty about to be erased. **Read the field; do not overwrite it with a narrative — in either direction.**

### S5 · Four unmarked movements in 99 pages
Giuliano ×3 (speaks at p. 17 with no entrance since p. 9; "comes in" at p. 40 with no exit written; on again at p. 79), Lydia ×1 (speaks at p. 69 with no entrance since p. 59). **Every placement is an inference written into the unit body as a flagged judgement call.** Expect ~1 per 20 pages. **The strict rule is right AND the pass cannot be mechanical.**

### S6 · Container misreads — three in one session
Read a task's `folderName` and reported a folder as a sibling when it was a subfolder. **A `folderName` gives the immediate parent and says NOTHING about depth.** Same species as reading a schema instead of the rows, and reading the script instead of the production. **Open the container.**

### S7 · A field that exists is not a field that inherits
`Production Note` is defined at LIST scope on Show Design, so it does **not** appear on a sibling list in the same folder. Caught before Michael hit it. **Promote to folder scope; never rebuild a second copy of a live vocabulary.**

### S8 · The part-file seam
When a script is split into parts, a unit routinely straddles the boundary. Big Love's part 2/part 3 seam fell mid-speech (Giuliano's ribbon → Barbies). **Number provisionally, reconcile when the adjacent part is read, and never let a file boundary create a unit boundary.**

## 🔬 Patterns worth carrying to the next script

**1. Physical set-pieces come in MIRRORED SETS.** Big Love: U11 (women) and U20 (men) are the same construction — synchronized collapsing, overlapping shouted text, drowning music — and U28 is where they converge. **Three instances of one species needing one specialist. That is a single line on the season calendar, not three staging notes.** Look for the mirror.

**2. The deck accumulates.** Big Love ran FIVE contaminants across one performance: glass (U04) → tomato pulp (U05) → cake (U28) → blood (U28) → rice (U29), with barefoot dancing between several of them. **Track the chain, not the incidents.**

**3. `People` demands hide in plain sight.** They are the type most often absent from every other production list because they have no drawing and no purchase order — intimacy director, fight call, choreographers, dialect coach, a pianist. On Big Love, eight of them.

**4. A playwright's optional menu is a scoping decision with a hazard budget.** Mee's *"not these things necessarily, but things like these"* hands the production an open list of extreme actions. **Somebody chooses, and it must happen before design.**

**5. Verbatim repeats are intentional.** Mee reuses `"Giuliano, mi dispiacce, ma..."` at p. 17 (over tomatoes) and p. 85 (over corpses). **Flag a repeat to the director as a rhyme, not a typo.**

**6. A full-stage reset can be written as dialogue.** Big Love's U29 clears bodies, blood, cake and debris while the trial proceeds. **Run-crew choreography disguised as scenery, and it lands on nobody's plot unless the ledger catches it.**
