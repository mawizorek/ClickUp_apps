## Ledger C — Standards that keep mattering

**EMPTY, and this one is under the strictest rule in the fleet.** Nothing enters here without
**body + designation + clause + edition/year**, or it enters carrying the literal word
`unverified`. **A fabricated ANSI clause reads exactly like a real one** — there is no formatting
tell, no plausibility check, and the reader cannot catch it. Only the writer can.
Three banned shapes: inventing a designation · inventing a clause inside a REAL standard (worse,
because the outer half verifies) · quoting a remembered edition as current.
(`gates/craft-guardrails.md` §1.)

## Ledger D — Questions he keeps getting asked (the service-provider ledger)

✅ **OPENED 2026-09-21. This was the first real question put to him, seven weeks after he was built.**

### D-1 · Big Love (F26) · *"Get the motor labels, truss labels and pick-point floor labels together."*

**Asked 01:14. Owed 10:00. The same morning the scenery load-in started.** The truss drawing it
names had been sitting at rev A since 14 SEP, and the naming question had been answerable for a
week. ⭐ **Per this ledger's own shape, the lateness IS the entry** — the question was not hard, it
was simply asked nine hours before it was due instead of at the design meeting.

🔑 **THE CRAFT FINDING, and the reason this is a ledger entry rather than a log line: A LABEL IS NOT
A RIGGING QUESTION.** Naming twenty-one points requires **zero capacities** — a name is not a rating,
so the entire label deliverable can be produced without him and without a single load number.
⚠️ **And he still gets seated, for the inverse reason: a label sheet is precisely where an unsourced
number gets written down.** Somebody labelling a pick point is one keystroke from putting a working
load beside it.

🎯 **So his function on a labelling task is to police what must NOT appear, not to supply what must.**
That is a different posture from the one the dependency table implies, and it will recur on every
paperwork deliverable this trade touches.

⭐ **THE STRUCTURAL FIX WORTH CARRYING FORWARD**, proposed by the file-discipline teammate in the same
session: put **`Source Plate` as a FIELD on the record**, beside the token, rather than as a note in
prose. **A worksheet shows an empty cell; prose hides a missing sentence.** 🔑 That turns *"never state
a capacity you did not read"* from a discipline somebody has to remember into **a column that is
visibly blank when the source is missing.** **Ask for this field on any schedule that will carry a
rigging number.**

⚠️ **Open at time of writing, and Michael's to answer:** the bridle count needed against the four
already in usable stock. **A count is a house-inventory fact and does not belong in this file** —
recorded only as *the shape of question that arrived attached to a labelling request*, which is the
pattern: **a paperwork ask usually has a materials decision hiding inside it.**
