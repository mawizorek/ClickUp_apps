**What happened.** Seated by Mira into the full-company read of the script demand ledger (30 units,
34 demands). I held no questions from anyone, so I read the paperwork instead — and found **two rows
tagged `Rigging` that were not rigging**: the body pit (a hole in a deck) and the wheeled building (a

dynamically, and it stops by hitting something. Michael ruled the split in the morning: **pit is
Randy's, rolling unit is mine.**

**What it taught the craft.**

1. ⭐ **ALTITUDE IS NOT THE TEST. LOAD PATH IS.** I had been reasoning from *does it leave the
   ground*, which is the most common instance of my lane and not the definition of it. It answered
   correctly for the pit and wrongly for the rolling unit, and *"it's on the floor so it isn't
   Gable's"* is precisely the reasoning that puts a caster through a deck. **The dependency wording
   was corrected in the shared contract the same night (§5a, PR #804), not just in my head.**
2. ⭐ **A DECLARED DEPENDENCY WRITTEN AROUND ITS MOST COMMON INSTANCE FAILS SILENTLY ON THE FIRST
   UNCOMMON ONE.** Nothing fires, nobody is seated, and **the gap looks like agreement.** This is
   the generalizable half and it is not about rigging.
3. **AN ABSENCE OF ROWS IS NOT AN ABSENCE OF WORK.** Zero flown elements on a 34-demand ledger has
   two readings and only one of them is safe to assume. **Say which one you are betting on and
   why.**
4. **A LABEL THAT LOOKS STAFFED IS WORSE THAN A BLANK ONE.** The fireworks row carried
   `Lighting (LX)` — which routes it to a real department and makes an unowned hazard read as
   assigned. The flashbulbs row carried no electrics tag at all. **Backed Vinny on both.**
5. ⚠️ **I WAS NOT ASKED ANYTHING.** For a service provider that is the load-bearing observation of
   the whole session, and it is a routing fact rather than a lane fact — **the same shape as
